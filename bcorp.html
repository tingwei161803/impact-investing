B Corps and B Lab

How three friends turned the lessons of a sneaker company's sale into a global certification for business as a force for good.

01 · Origins

From sneakers to a movement

B Lab began with a basketball-shoe company and a question about what happens to a mission when a company is sold.

Before B Lab there was AND 1, a basketball footwear and apparel brand with roughly US$250 million in revenue, known for treating its workers and supply chain unusually well. Jay Coen Gilbert co-founded it, Bart Houlahan ran it as president, and Andrew Kassoy — a private equity investor — backed it. The three had been friends and roommates at Stanford.

When AND 1 was sold in 2005, the three saw how quickly a company's values can unravel once it changes hands. The question of how a business can stay true to its purpose through growth and new owners led them to found the nonprofit B Lab in Berwyn, Pennsylvania in 2006, under the credo of business as a force for good.

  1. 2006

    B Lab founded

    Gilbert, Houlahan and Kassoy establish the nonprofit in Berwyn, Pennsylvania.

  2. 2007

    First 81 B Corps

    The first cohort includes an employee-owned flour company founded in 1790, a cleaning-products startup, a clog maker, a yerba mate importer and a Philadelphia café.

  3. 2010

    Maryland writes it into law

    On 13 April, Governor Martin O'Malley signs SB 690, making Maryland the first US state with a benefit corporation statute.

  4. 2016

    Società Benefit in Italy

    Italy becomes the first European country — and the second worldwide after the US — to recognize benefit corporations in law.

  5. 2025

    New standards

    On 8 April, B Lab publishes its largest-ever overhaul of the certification, replacing the 80-point score with mandatory requirements.

  6. 2026

    First certifications under the new bar

    On 16 June, the first nine companies — including Ricola, Rituals, Lumene and Nativa — certify under the new standards; the global community passes 10,800 companies in 102 countries.

02 · Mechanics

How certification works

A verified assessment, a legal commitment, and — since 2025 — mandatory requirements instead of a score.

Certification is granted company by company. Under the system used until 2025, an applicant completed the B Impact Assessment, had its answers verified, embedded stakeholder interests in its legal documents, and recertified every three years.

New standards, April 2025: on 8 April 2025 B Lab published its most sweeping revision yet. The 80-point score is gone; companies must instead meet Foundation Requirements plus specific mandatory requirements across seven Impact Topics — Purpose & Stakeholder Governance, Climate Action, Human Rights, Fair Work, Environmental Stewardship & Circularity, Justice/Equity/Diversity/Inclusion (JEDI), and Government Affairs & Collective Action — with improvement milestones at years 0, 3 and 5. Recertification under the old standard closed on 30 June 2025; from 2026, new applicants certify under version 2.1.

03 · Law

Certification vs. legislation

A Certified B Corp is not the same thing as a benefit corporation — one is a label, the other a legal form.

The two are constantly confused. Certification is granted by the private nonprofit B Lab after verified assessment; a benefit corporation is a corporate form written into state or national company law, with no performance test attached. A company can be either, both, or neither.

06 · Debate

Controversies and criticism

The sharpest challenges to the label have come from inside the community.

Two flashpoints — Nespresso's certification and Dr. Bronner's exit — frame a broader argument about whether the standard is strict enough to mean anything.

  • Nespresso open letter (2022): after Nespresso certified in April 2022, Fair World Project led an open letter signed by more than 20 B Corps, including Dr. Bronner's and specialty coffee roasters. It cited a 2020 investigation that found children as young as eight working on seven Guatemalan farms supplying Nespresso, wage-withholding cases uncovered in Brazil in 2021, and the environmental impact of single-use aluminium capsules — and demanded that human-rights abuses become automatically disqualifying, with a tougher BIA overall.
  • Dr. Bronner's exits (2025): the organic soap maker — certified since 2015 and holder of the highest BIA score ever recorded, 206.7 — announced in February 2025 that it would not recertify, arguing that B Lab lets multinationals such as Unilever and Nespresso/Nestlé certify without third-party verification of their supply chains, turning the label into a marketing tool that contradicts its mission. UK pet-food brand Scrumbles dropped the certification the same year.
  • Structural criticism: the certification has no legal standing, no mandatory due-diligence mechanism, and relies on company-reported data — leaving room for greenwashing. Researchers have also questioned how firmly certification links to sustainability outcomes.
  • B Lab's answer: the 2025 standards — scrapping flexible scoring in favor of mandatory human-rights and climate requirements — are widely read as a direct response to these critiques and to EU rules such as the ECGT directive.

07 · Taiwan

B Corps in Taiwan

A quick snapshot — the full ecosystem has its own page.

Taiwan counts 63 certified B Corps across wholesale, manufacturing, finance, education, technology and food service. DOMI Earth became the island's first in June 2014; O-Bank, certified in October 2017, was the first publicly listed bank in the world to earn the label.

The full Taiwan story — B Lab Taiwan (founded 2016 as B Lab's first Asian partner), Greenvines, Chien Wei CPAs and the wider ecosystem — is covered on the Taiwan page (taiwan.html).
