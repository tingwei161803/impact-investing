Impact Investing & B Corps

A bilingual field guide to investing that pursues measurable social and environmental impact alongside financial return — the ideas, the companies, the countries and the people. Every fact carries a reference.

Impact investing sits between traditional investing and philanthropy: capital deployed with the explicit intention of generating positive, measurable social and environmental impact alongside a financial return. The B Corp movement asks a related question from the company side — can a business be certified, governed and rebuilt to serve all stakeholders, not only shareholders?

This site collects the essentials of both movements: the core concepts and their history, the B Corp certification system, Muhammad Yunus and Grameen Bank, examples from ten countries and regions, the key figures who defined the field, and Taiwan's own ecosystem. Sources are linked throughout; the full research notes live in the repository.

Origin

Why this site exists

At a youth innovation summit in Taipei on 2026-07-19, Ray Chen — co-founder and president of B Current Impact Investment, Taiwan's first VC management firm investing 100% in social-innovation enterprises — gave a talk on impact investing: B Corps, Muhammad Yunus and the "bank for the poor", and the idea that today's social innovation becomes tomorrow's business as usual.

That talk raised more questions than an afternoon could answer. This site is the follow-up homework: a systematic, referenced tour of the field he sketched — from the 2007 Bellagio meeting that coined the term "impact investing", to the villages of Bangladesh, to Taiwan's own funds, certifiers and startups.
